## 1. Executive Summary

### Recommended Licensing Strategy in 5 Bullets

1. **RAG stays on Posture A** — Continue the "Embed + Cite, never redistribute raw PDFs" model for the existing ~2,707-PDF corpus. This posture has the strongest fair use footing (Google Books precedent), and the 2025 Bartz v. Anthropic ruling reinforces that lawful acquisition + transformative use = fair use. No change needed. Do not weaken this posture by exposing raw PDF content.

2. **Buy access, don't scrape** — Purchase annual subscriptions to **Whirlpool ServiceMatters (~$300/yr minimum)** and **Appliantology Pro (~$100-200/yr)** for legitimate, credentialed corpus expansion. These cover ~35-40% of the US market (Whirlpool family) plus a curated multi-brand archive. Frame access under Right to Repair — Minnesota's DFRA (effective 2024) requires manufacturers to provide documentation at no charge to owners and independent repair providers. This is the single most impactful legal lever HomeOps has.

3. **Fine-tune ONLY on synthesized/derived data, not raw manuals** — This is the non-negotiable posture for training. The training data must be instruction pairs generated by a cloud LLM from extracted factual content (error codes, symptom→fix patterns), not raw manual text. Facts are not copyrightable (Feist v. Rural). The cloud-LLM synthesis step adds a transformative layer. ⚖️ Get IP attorney sign-off before any training run.

4. **Negotiate B2B data licenses with Parts Town/Encompass for structured diagnostic data** — Parts Town (which acquired Encompass in 2022) has the richest structured repair data in the industry: parts catalogs, model-to-part mappings, symptom-to-repair patterns from millions of historical repairs. A license for this structured data (not raw PDFs) would dramatically accelerate model-depth coverage. The corpus build plan estimates $5K-$50K/yr. Call them: the data is more valuable for HomeOps' diagnostic use case than raw manuals.

5. **Pilot a manufacturer partnership with 1-2 brands under Right-to-Repair framing** — Approach Whirlpool and GE Appliances not as a scraper/competitor but as a Right-to-Repair infrastructure partner. Pitch: "HomeOps helps consumers self-diagnose before calling a technician, reducing unnecessary warranty claims and improving first-time fix rates for your authorized service network." Both companies have developer/partner programs (Whirlpool Developer Portal, GE SmartHQ Developer Portal) — though these are IoT/smart-home APIs, not content APIs, they are entry points to the right BD contacts.

### 12-Month Cost Estimate

**Mid scenario: $24,700** (Low: $6,100 / High: $82,200). See §4 for detailed line items.

### 3 Biggest Open Questions

1. **Will any manufacturer actually license their service manual corpus for AI training/RAG?** No major appliance manufacturer has a public content-licensing program. Their developer portals are IoT/smart-home APIs, not documentation APIs. The path is through BD/partnership conversations, not self-serve portals. The outcome could range from "enthusiastic partner" to "cease and desist." We won't know until we ask.

2. **Does the "synthesized data only" fine-tuning strategy withstand legal scrutiny?** The Bartz v. Anthropic ruling (June 2025) found training on lawfully acquired works to be fair use, but that was pre-training, not domain-specific fine-tuning. No court has ruled on whether LLM-synthesized training data derived from copyrighted sources constitutes a derivative work. This is the single largest legal risk in the entire HomeOps product strategy.

3. **Is Parts Town/Encompass willing to license structured diagnostic data to an AI startup, and at what price?** The $5K-$50K estimate from the corpus build plan is a placeholder. Parts Town is a $2B+ company that acquired Encompass to expand its residential division — they may see HomeOps as a competitor or a partner. The pricing and terms are entirely unknown until a conversation happens.

| **New York** | ✅ 2023 | Electronics focus; appliances explicitly excluded | N/A for appliances | **Low.** |
| **Federal REPAIR Act (H.R.1566)** | Pending — subcommittee advanced Feb 2026 | Motor vehicles (not appliances) | Would mandate manufacturer access | **Monitor.** Automotive-focused, but signals federal momentum. |
| **Federal Fair Repair Act (H.R.7404)** | Introduced Feb 2026 | Digital electronics | Parts, tools, documentation on fair terms. Prohibits parts pairing. | **Monitor.** Would be transformative if passed. |
| **2026 R2R Template (Repair Association)** | Published 2026 | Broad digital electronic equipment | Expanded tool access (software, data files, activation mechanisms, security credentials). Offline-capable tools required. | **Trend indicator.** The template laws are getting stronger each year. |

**Strategic framing:** Minnesota's DFRA is the anchor. It requires manufacturers of digital electronic equipment (including home appliances) to provide documentation at no charge to independent repair providers. HomeOps, as a diagnostic platform serving consumers and independent technicians, fits squarely within the law's intended beneficiary class. Frame every manufacturer conversation with: "We're not asking for a favor — Minnesota, Colorado, Oregon, and 20+ other states already require this. We're offering to be the infrastructure platform that makes compliance easy for you."

## 3. Recommended Strategy — Tiered Approach

├── Ingest CPSC + EnergyStar + DOE data (public domain, free)

TIER 2 (Month 3-6): EXPAND LICENSED COVERAGE
├── Negotiate Parts Town/Encompass data license for structured diagnostic data
├── Approach iFixit for commercial AI training license
├── Miele Pro registration (free)

├── Internet Archive pre-1978 vintage manuals (public domain)

TIER 3 (Month 6-12): MANUFACTURER PARTNERSHIP + TRAINING

### 3.2 Tier 1 — Solidify the Baseline (Now, Month 0-3)

**What:** Establish the legally cleanest possible foundation before any training run.

**RAG corpus posture (no change):**
- Continue Posture A (Embed + Cite) for the existing ~2,707 PDF corpus.
- This posture is supported by Google Books precedent and reinforced by the June 2025 Bartz v. Anthropic ruling, which found training on lawfully acquired works to be "spectacularly transformative" fair use.
- **Action:** Add a `license_manifest.jsonl` to the corpus tracking source and license posture for every document. The data-prep plan §4.5.1 already specifies this.

**Purchased access (credentials-based, no ToS violation):**
- **Appliantology Pro:** ~$100-200/yr. Instant access to a curated multi-brand manual archive. While commercial use terms need review, accessing manuals as a professional member for RAG ingestion is fundamentally similar to how thousands of technicians use the forum daily. The corpus is better for having credentialed access to it.
- **Whirlpool ServiceMatters:** ~$300/yr minimum. The single highest-ROI paid access — covers ~35-40% of US installed appliances across the Whirlpool family. Requires: business entity (InTrades LLC exists), EIN, GL insurance cert (~$50-100/mo), background checks. This is legitimate paid access — not scraping, not faking credentials.

**Public domain ingestion (zero legal risk):**
- CPSC Recall API — appliance recalls with make/model/date. Pull the full database.
- EnergyStar Product Database — 200K+ certified models. Already planned for Phase 0.
- DOE Appliance Standards DB — energy efficiency by model. Phase 2 enrichment.

**Legal review:**
- ⚖️ Engage an IP attorney for: (1) review of current corpus ingestion posture, (2) opinion letter on fine-tuning-with-synthesized-data strategy, (3) review of ServiceMatters/Appliantology ToS for commercial diagnostic use.
- **Cost estimate:** $5,000-15,000 for initial review + opinion letter. Do not skip this — the cost of getting it wrong (retraining, takedown, litigation) is orders of magnitude higher.

**Outreach drafts:**
- Draft templated emails to: Whirlpool (BD/partnerships), GE Appliances (SmartHQ Pro BD), Parts Town/Encompass (data licensing), iFixit (legal@ifixit.com for commercial AI license).
- Lead with Right to Repair framing, not "we want your data." Pitch: infrastructure partner for R2R compliance + consumer self-diagnosis reducing unnecessary service calls.

### 3.3 Tier 2 — Expand Licensed Coverage (Month 3-6)

**Parts Town / Encompass data license:**
- **What to license:** Structured diagnostic data — model-to-part mappings, symptom-to-repair patterns, failure-frequency data. NOT raw PDFs.
- **Why this instead of raw manuals:** Structured diagnostic data (error codes, symptoms, parts, fixes) is higher-signal for both RAG and training than prose manual text. It's also likely cheaper to license because Parts Town's core business is parts distribution, not manual publishing.
- **Pricing:** Unknown until negotiated. The corpus build plan estimates $5K-$50K/yr. Mid-range assumption: $15,000-25,000/yr for a startup-friendly tier.
- **Key contact:** Parts Town Unlimited (parent company), Encompass Solutions division. The 2025 ServicePower partnership announcement shows they do B2B data deals.

**iFixit commercial AI license:**
- iFixit's April 2026 ToS update explicitly states: "Training AI — Using our data to train a machine learning or AI model is a violation of our terms of use." But their Licensing page says they "grant usage licenses to certain commercial entities, including for training large language models."
- **Action:** Email legal@ifixit.com. Ask for: commercial license for RAG ingestion + fine-tuning training data, appliance-specific content only, startup-friendly terms.
- **Pricing:** Not publicly listed. Estimate $5,000-25,000/yr based on typical API/data licensing for a company of iFixit's scale (~$97M revenue).
- **Note:** Even with a commercial license, iFixit's appliance coverage is thin (they're electronics-focused). The value is in their structured repair guide format, which is a template for HomeOps' own diagnostic content.

**Open-access manufacturer portals:**
- **Miele Pro:** Self-register at mielepro.com as a trade professional. Free. Lowest friction of all major brands. Covers ~3% of US market (premium segment).
- **Speed Queen:** Manuals publicly available at speedqueen.com with relatively lenient ToS. Covers ~2% of US market (commercial/residential laundry).

**Public domain vintage manuals:**
- Internet Archive: Pre-1978 manuals are likely public domain (published without copyright notice, or copyright not renewed). While vintage, these provide baseline coverage for older appliances still in service.
- **Caveat:** Verify copyright status per document. The Archive's own position is that they operate under library exceptions, but individual document copyright varies.

**Begin synthesized training data construction:**
- Using ONLY data from licensed sources (ServiceMatters, Appliantology, public domain) plus extracted factual content (error codes, symptom patterns from RAG-accessible corpus), begin constructing instruction-tuning pairs via cloud LLM synthesis.
- This is the bridge from "corpus of PDFs" to "training dataset." See data-prep plan §4.3 for methodology.

### 3.4 Tier 3 — Manufacturer Partnership + Training (Month 6-12)

**Manufacturer partnership pilot:**
- **Target:** 1-2 receptive manufacturers. Whirlpool (largest US market share, existing developer portal) and GE Appliances (SmartHQ Pro program, "Elevate Your Vision" BD outreach) are the best first calls.
- **Pitch deck:** "HomeOps: Right-to-Repair Infrastructure for Appliance Diagnostics." Frame as: (1) consumer self-diagnosis reduces unnecessary warranty claims, (2) structured diagnostic handoff improves first-time fix rates for authorized service networks, (3) HomeOps is the platform, not the repairer — we don't compete with your service network.
- **What to ask for:** API access to service documentation (not just IoT control), error code databases, model/variant mapping data. Ideally: a content license for diagnostic use.
- **Fallback position:** If no API/content license is available, ask for: (a) a letter of non-objection to RAG-based use of publicly available documentation for diagnostic purposes, (b) introduction to the right BD/legal contact for future partnership.

**Fine-tune Maple-Preview on synthesized data:**
- Only after: (1) IP attorney sign-off on training data provenance, (2) training dataset is 100% traceable to licensed/public-domain/synthesized sources, (3) held-out eval set is built (data-prep plan P1-W5).
- **Model:** Maple-Preview (20B-A1B MoE, MIT license) via standard LoRA/QLoRA. Bonsai 8B as phone-sized spike only.
- **Training data composition:**
  - Public domain: CPSC recall text, EnergyStar specs, DOE standards
  - Licensed: Parts Town structured diagnostic data (if license obtained), iFixit guides (if commercial license obtained)
  - Synthesized/derived: Instruction pairs generated by cloud LLM from extracted factual error codes, symptom→fix patterns, diagnostic decision trees
  - **Absolutely excluded:** Raw manufacturer manual text, scraped ManualsLib content, Internet Archive post-1978 manuals with unclear copyright

**Annual review and renewal:**
- Reassess legal landscape: federal R2R legislation status, AI training case law developments (NYT v. OpenAI ruling expected; Kadrey/Meta appeal; Anthropic class action settlement implementation)
- Renew subscriptions (ServiceMatters, Appliantology)
- Evaluate: expand to Samsung/LG/Bosch portals via authorized-servicer partnership with an existing multi-brand service company

### 3.5 What We Explicitly Do NOT Do

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Scrape ManualsLib or any aggregator | ToS violation. No clean licensing path. Poisons corpus provenance. |
| Fake technician credentials for Samsung/LG/GE/Bosch portals | The manufacturer portal access doc is explicit: "Do NOT fake technician status: false-pretenses access violates ToS and poisons the corpus's licensing posture — fatal for a product whose pitch is auditable trust." |
| Fine-tune on raw manual text | Maximum legal risk. Even if fair use ultimately prevails (Bartz precedent is favorable), the litigation risk is too high for a pre-revenue startup. Synthesized data only. |
| Use OpenRepairData in training data | CC BY-SA 4.0's ShareAlike condition creates unresolved copyleft risk for commercial model weights. Use for analytics/failure-frequency analysis only. |
| Redistribute raw PDFs | Posture A is "Embed + Cite, never redistribute." This is the cornerstone of the fair use position. Do not erode it. |
| Skip legal review | The cost of getting licensing wrong is existential. $5-15K for an IP attorney opinion letter is the cheapest insurance HomeOps will ever buy. |

## 6. Risks & Open Questions

### 6.1 Risk Register

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| R1 | **Manufacturer C&D over RAG corpus ingestion** | Low-Medium | High — could force corpus takedown | Posture A (Embed + Cite, never redistribute) is the strongest fair use footing. Google Books precedent + Bartz v. Anthropic ruling support transformative use of lawfully acquired works. Minnesota DFRA requires documentation access. |
| R2 | **Synthesized-data fine-tuning found to be derivative work** | Medium | High — could force model retraining or takedown | Use only factual extractions (Feist: facts not copyrightable). Cloud LLM synthesis adds transformative layer. Attorney opinion letter before training. Worst case: fall back to RAG-only with licensed/public domain corpus only. |
| R3 | **No manufacturer willing to license documentation** | Medium-High | Medium — limits corpus completeness but doesn't block product | Alternate paths exist: ServiceMatters/Appliantology (paid access), Parts Town (structured data), public domain + open-licensed sources. Product can ship on RAG with current corpus + licensed expansion. |
| R4 | **Parts Town/Encompass license prohibitively expensive** | Medium | Medium — reduces structured data quality for training | Fall back to public domain + extracted-fact + synthesized data only. The training data is "nice to have," not "must have" for Phase 1 RAG product. |
| R5 | **iFixit refuses commercial AI license or prices it out of reach** | Medium | Low-Medium — iFixit appliance coverage is thin anyway | iFixit is valuable for structured repair guide format, not for coverage volume. Fall back to internal guide authoring + public domain sources. |
| R6 | **Federal R2R legislation passes with strong appliance coverage** | Low-Medium (2026) | Positive — would mandate manufacturer access | Monitor and be ready to leverage immediately. Would transform the licensing landscape from "persuade manufacturers" to "demand compliance." |
| R7 | **NYT v. OpenAI ruling restricts fair use for AI training** | Low-Medium | High — would weaken Bartz precedent | Synthesized-data-only strategy is designed to survive even an adverse fair use ruling. Structured facts + cloud LLM synthesis = two layers of transformation. |
| R8 | **ServiceMatters ToS prohibits use for third-party diagnostic products** | Medium | Medium — blocks the best single paid access point | Attorney review of ToS BEFORE subscribing. If prohibited, escalate to Whirlpool BD for a custom license. Minnesota DFRA may provide leverage. |
| R9 | **Appliantology Pro ToS prohibits commercial use of downloaded manuals** | Medium | Medium — blocks the cheapest multi-brand access | Attorney review of ToS. If prohibited, use Appliantology for research/reference only while pursuing direct manufacturer licenses. |
